How Oblivia works

Who may ask, under which statute, at what age-boundary.

Oblivia is a rights lifecycle protocol for a parent or legal guardian to erase a child's data trail under a specific privacy statute — with a receipt — instead of a blind "delete my profile" app hook.

Flows

Enroll: guardian opens a protected subject bound to a live policy version (jurisdiction, self-erase age, retention cap, profiling ban). Pseudonym only, the wallet out only hashes. Chain stores NO personal data.

Consent & data: memories are encrypted in the guardian's browser (AES-256-GCM), hub stores only ciphertext. Guardian commits the data-root on chain.

Erase: guardian requests (wallet tx = authority proof) → op queued → notarized (Chainlink DON when CRE is configured, hub key otherwise) → on-chain receipt → DEC v2 certificate hash pinned on chain (item nullifiers recomputed from ciphertext hash, all shred done only AFTER the receipt confirms) → hub wipes the ciphertext it held.

Stages the queue can be in

  • awaiting guardian txn (entry created, no authority in hub yet)
  • queued for notary (guardian tx seen)
  • notarized (ATTESTOR) / report sent (DON) (executor fired)
  • confirmed on chain (receipt verified → DEC issued + shred)

What is / is NOT stored on chain

On chain: statute-bound bindings, hashed commitments, DEC hashes, lifecycle transitions and wallet authority hand-offs. NOT on chain: any plaintext, any PII.

Trust model

Guardian funds gas. Hub has no guardian key. The attestor key signs only what a wallet already authorized. Anything the hub writes it published publicly on chain.

Change engine

Rule tables live in PolicyRegistry — jurisdictions can evolve without contract redeploy (semver per jurisdiction), historical versions remain addressable to prove what was in force at time of enrollment.
